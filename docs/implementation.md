# How ipldt reimplements the IPL evaluation, stage by stage

ipldt (1.0.0) is a Python package that reimplements the IPL standard bone morphometry evaluation for XtremeCT II images; ORMIR-BQRL (0.1.0) wraps it into a workflow that takes a Scanco AIM file and returns masks, maps, and a report without user interaction ([Figure 1](../manuscript/figures/fig1_workflow.png)). [How the reimplementation was derived](derivation.md) describes how the rules were fixed. The stages are described below in pipeline order, with the IPL commands they reimplement in parentheses. Every parameter, with its IPL option name, its value in the tibia and radius presets, what it controls, and the documentation panel that shows its effect on real or synthetic data, is listed in the [parameter table](../manuscript/tables/tableS1_parameters.md), and the panels are collected in [Documentation Figures D1–D9](figures.md). References to Figures 1–6 are to the main figures of the paper, which are kept in [`manuscript/figures/`](../manuscript/figures/).

## AIM input and output

The package reads Scanco AIM files directly: the native int16 grayscale with its header and processing log, and the char, run-length-compressed and bit-compressed products IPL writes (masks, segmentations, maps). The density calibration IPL uses (slope, intercept, and µ-scaling) is read from the processing log, so thresholds given in mg HA/cm³ are converted to native units per scan ([Figure D1B](figures.md#figure-d1)). Volumes keep their global voxel position and are combined or compared by position. Output is written as AIM, with the input header, or as NIfTI.

## Periosteal contour

The periosteal contour is produced by the ORMIR-XCT autocontour (1.1.0), an implementation (Kuczynski et al. 2024) of the dual-threshold method of Buie et al. (2007), called with the calibration constants of the scan. It is the one stage that is not a reimplementation of an IPL operation and is cited rather than re-validated. A periosteal contour can also be supplied as a file.

## Cortical/trabecular compartment separation

IPL separates the two compartments from the grayscale and the periosteal contour in a chain of 36 IPL commands (STEP 1 of the evaluation script; [Figure 2](../manuscript/figures/fig2_step1.png)): Gaussian smoothing and thresholding of dense bone (/seg_gauss, σ = 2.0, 500–3,000 mg HA/cm³; [Figure D1](figures.md#figure-d1)); subtraction from the periosteal region with a peel (/gobj_maskaimpeel_ow; [D2H](figures.md#figure-d2), [D3A](figures.md#figure-d3)); hole filling and largest-component extraction (/cl_ow_rank_extract; [D3B](figures.md#figure-d3)); erosion, dilation, closing, and opening ([D2D–G](figures.md#figure-d2), [D3B–D](figures.md#figure-d3)); a corner branch that restores thin cortical corners from the difference of the closed and opened masks (/cl_nr_extract; [D3E, F](figures.md#figure-d3)); a second closing with a peel ([D3E–G](figures.md#figure-d3)); and slice-wise extraction of the components that cover at least half of each slice (/cl_slicewise_extractow; [D3H](figures.md#figure-d3)), for the trabecular mask and then for the cortical mask, the periosteal region minus the trabecular mask. ipldt reimplements each command with IPL's chamfer metric and distance threshold (metric 11, weights 3-4-5 (Borgefors 1986), raw < 3N + 2; [D2A–C](figures.md#figure-d2)) and its boundary conventions ([D2I–L](figures.md#figure-d2)), 6-connected components in three dimensions and 4-connected slice-wise ([D3B, H](figures.md#figure-d3)). Every distance, threshold, and component bound, and the two site presets, which differ only in the corner-component threshold and the second closing distance, are given in the [parameter table](../manuscript/tables/tableS1_parameters.md) ([D3E–G](figures.md#figure-d3)).

## Contour rendering

IPL stores every mask as a contour object, and every masked command sees the rasterization of that contour rather than the mask. ipldt reimplements the conversion of a mask to a contour (/togobj_from_aim -curvature_smooth 1), which traces the components and holes of each slice ([Figure D4D](figures.md#figure-d4)), smooths the chains with IPL's gated curvature rule ([D4E, F, I](figures.md#figure-d4)), and discards chains below IPL's minimum vertex count ([D4J](figures.md#figure-d4)), and the rasterization back to a volume by even-odd filling (/gobj_to_aim; [D4G, H](figures.md#figure-d4)); a rendering can differ from the mask it came from ([D4A–C](figures.md#figure-d4)). The rendered periosteal, cortical and trabecular contours mask the segmentation and the distance transforms, as in IPL.

## Laplace–Hamming segmentation

Mineralized bone is segmented from the native grayscale with IPL's Laplace–Hamming filter (Laib and Rüegsegger 1999) (STEP 2; [Figure 3](../manuscript/figures/fig3_lh.png)). ipldt reimplements the sequence: a one-voxel duplicated border and mirror padding of each axis to the next power of two, with the extra voxel before the data when the pad is odd ([Figure D5D](figures.md#figure-d5)); the filter in the three-dimensional Fourier domain (/fft_laplace_hamming, ε = 0.45, low-pass cutoff 0.3 of the sampling frequency along the slice axis, window amplitude 1; [D5A–C](figures.md#figure-d5)) with the per-axis element sizes of the header ([Figure 3A](../manuscript/figures/fig3_lh.png)), in single precision; scaling of ±200,000 to the int16 range with truncation (/norm_max); and thresholding at 475–1,000‰ of that range (/threshold; 15,564 to 32,767; [D5E](figures.md#figure-d5)). The result is masked with the periosteal contour, cleaned of components of fewer than 35 voxels for the cortical and 70 for the trabecular part (/cl_nr_extract; [D5F](figures.md#figure-d5)), each part then cropped to its rendered compartment contour, and combined into IPL's SEG with labels 127 (cortical) and 126 (trabecular). Despite the command's name, the window is not a Hamming window: the amplitude of 1 that IPL records in its log makes the cosine term 0.5 + 0.5 cos(π|k|/k_c), a Hann window; a Hamming window would require 0.92 ([D5C](figures.md#figure-d5)).

## Cortical pore extraction

IPL extracts the intracortical pore space from the cortical segmentation and the cortical contour by the cascade of Burghardt et al. (2010) (STEP 2; [Figure D9](figures.md#figure-d9)). ipldt reimplements it command for command: a first pore estimate, the voids of the cortical segmentation that fill no more than 5% of their slice (/cl_slicewise_extractow; [D9B](figures.md#figure-d9)); removal of the voids continuous with the largest component of everything that is not cortical bone, masked back to the cortical contour (/cl_rank_extract, /gobj_maskaimpeel_ow; [D9C](figures.md#figure-d9)); a hysteresis along the slice axis that recovers voids the slice-wise rule missed where they continue in z into non-bone space outside the compartment (/hysteresis_threshold, thresholds 1 and 3 in native units, low-intensity object, growth along z only; [D9D–F](figures.md#figure-d9)); a second slice-wise pass; and removal of components smaller than 20 voxels (/cl_nr_extract; [D9G](figures.md#figure-d9)).

## Distance-transform engine

IPL's dt_thickness, dt_spacing, and dt_number assign to every voxel of a binary object the diameter of the largest sphere that contains it and fits within the object, the definition of Hildebrand and Rüegsegger (1997) in IPL's discrete form (STEP 3; [Figure 4](../manuscript/figures/fig4_dt.png)). ipldt reimplements the chain: a vector distance transform with the outside of the image treated as object ([Figure D6A](figures.md#figure-d6)); the distance to the object surface, taken half a voxel inside the nearest background voxel ([D6B, C](figures.md#figure-d6)); the selection of sphere centers by a containment test against the 26 neighbors (ridge_epsilon 0.9; [D6D](figures.md#figure-d6)); the contour peel (peel_iter −1; [D6E](figures.md#figure-d6)); the integer diameter of every sphere by IPL's diameter rule 3 (-version 3; [D6G, H](figures.md#figure-d6)); the drawing of the spheres, each voxel taking the largest diameter of any sphere reaching it within assign_epsilon 0.5 ([D6I](figures.md#figure-d6)); and the clipping of the map to the contour and its statistics (suppress_boundary 2, which has no effect; [D6F](figures.md#figure-d6)). dt_spacing applies the chain to the marrow space of the segmentation ([D7A–D](figures.md#figure-d7)) and dt_number to the space between its ridges, ridge_epsilon entering twice ([D7E–H](figures.md#figure-d7)), as IPL does. The distance-transform sweep runs on the CPU (Numba); the containment test, the diameters, and the sphere drawing run on a GPU (CuPy) or on the CPU, and the two back ends return identical maps and centers and equal statistics ([Software environment and runtime](environment.md)).

## Metrics

The workflow reports BV/TV, the fraction of segmented voxels inside the trabecular contour; Tb.Th, the mean of the dt_thickness map of the whole segmentation (SEG) inside the trabecular contour ([Figure 4A](../manuscript/figures/fig4_dt.png)); Tb.Sp, the mean of the dt_spacing map of the marrow space of SEG ([Figure D7I](figures.md#figure-d7)); Tb.N, the reciprocal of the mean of the dt_number map ([D7J](figures.md#figure-d7)); and Ct.Th, the mean of the dt_thickness map of the cortical compartment inside the cortical contour ([D7M](figures.md#figure-d7)). Taking Tb.Th on SEG, like Tb.Sp and Tb.N, is deliberate: IPL's evaluation script takes it on the trabecular segmentation (TRAB_SEG, the trabecular part of SEG cropped to the trabecular contour; [D7K, L](figures.md#figure-d7)), which truncates a trabecula at the endocortical boundary, whereas on SEG its full width is measured. ipldt computes both, its dt_thickness taking either object, so users can choose; the comparison with IPL uses the script's definition. Trabecular and cortical BMD are computed with ORMIR-XCT bmd_masked inside the rendered contours (Kuczynski et al. 2024), and Ct.Po as the fraction of the cortical compartment occupied by the pore map.

## Outputs and manual-correction re-entry

Each run writes a JSON and CSV report (metrics, parameters, provenance of every mask), the masks, the pore map, and the distance-transform maps as NIfTI or AIM, and a 3D Slicer (Fedorov et al. 2012) segmentation (.seg.nrrd). A mask modified in 3D Slicer is read back onto the AIM lattice by physical position and re-enters the workflow with the semantics of IPL's re-evaluation script (Script 34): a modified periosteal contour re-runs the compartment separation; a modified compartment contour is rendered and taken as given, the other compartment being the periosteal region minus it.

## Every parameter

Every parameter of ipldt and ORMIR-BQRL, with the IPL option or command it corresponds to, its name in the package, its value (TIBIA / RADIUS where the two site presets differ), what it controls, and the panel that shows it, is listed in the [parameter table](../manuscript/tables/tableS1_parameters.md). The panel column of that table names the panels of [Documentation Figures D1–D9](figures.md) (D1A is panel A of Figure D1, and so on). The table's footnote states which of these values can be changed by the user and which rows describe fixed rules of a command.
